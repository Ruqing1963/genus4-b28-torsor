# Octads, O6+(2) and the genus-4 Gold family: a working memo

Ruqing Chen, GUT Geoservice, with Claude. 2026-10-11. Independent of the Legendre-interval repositories (Parts I–IV); nothing here is a theorem unless marked so, and every numerical statement is reproduced by `octad_combinatorics.py` and `genus_check.py` in this directory (outputs `octad_combinatorics.txt`, `genus_check.txt`).

## 0. Where this sits

This is **not Part V**. Part V, if written, continues the Legendre-interval series (class counts, class 3 2 1, identity (H) for class 6, even k). The present topic is a different object: the 63-point geometry of an octad, the orthogonal group O6+(2) ≅ S8 inside Sp6(F2), and the Artin–Schreier family whose polar operator has a six-dimensional kernel. It should be an independent exploration ("octad lab") and, if it matures, a separate paper that cites Part III for the hexad case.

Two premises of the task prompt had to be corrected by computation before anything else could be built on them; they are recorded in Section 2.

## 1. The combinatorial model (all statements verified exactly)

Let E = {e1, …, e8} with the single relation e1 + ⋯ + e8 = 0 in characteristic 2. The span is F2^8/⟨𝟙⟩; an element is a subset of E modulo complement. The even subsets modulo complement form a subspace V of dimension 6, and its 63 nonzero elements are exactly

| type | elements | count |
|---|---|---|
| duads | ei + ej | C(8,2) = 28 |
| tetrads | ei + ej + ek + el = sum of the complementary four | C(8,4)/2 = 35 |

Verified: the 63 elements together with 0 are closed under addition and span a 6-dimensional F2-space (`closed: True, dimension 6`).

Structure on V (verified):

* ⟨x, y⟩ = |x ∩ y| mod 2 is well defined modulo complement, alternating and nondegenerate: a symplectic form on V = F2^6.
* Q(x) = |x|/2 mod 2 is well defined (because 8 ≡ 0 mod 4), is a quadratic form with polar form ⟨,⟩, has 36 zeros including 0, hence Arf(Q) = 0: Q is of **plus type**. Its 35 singular points are the tetrads, its 28 nonsingular points the duads.
* S8 acts faithfully on V (image of order 40320), preserves ⟨,⟩ and Q, and has exactly two orbits on the 63 points, the duads and the tetrads. The transposition (i j) acts as the symplectic transvection at the duad ei + ej.
* The group generated by all 63 transvections has order 1451520 = |Sp6(F2)|, is transitive on the 63 points, contains S8 with index 36, and S8 is precisely the stabiliser of Q. Of the 64 quadratic forms with polar form ⟨,⟩, 36 are of plus type (one Sp6(F2)-orbit, stabiliser S8) and 28 of minus type.
* The affine coset (odd subsets modulo complement) has 64 points: the 8 singletons and the 56 triads ei + ej + ek (= complementary pentads). In the language of Part III this is the model of T'^{-1}(1) = r1 + K: eight "roots" and fifty-six "triad totals", replacing the six roots and ten synthematic totals of the hexad.

For comparison, N = 6 (`hexad_report`): 15 duads, V = F2^4, ⟨,⟩ well defined, but Q = |x|/2 is **not** well defined (a duad and its complementary tetrad would get 1 and 0), S6 = Sp4(F2) (both of order 720), and there is no S6-invariant quadratic form at all. The genus-2 theory of Parts III–IV therefore never sees an orthogonal group; the genus-4 theory will.

## 2. Corrections to the premise

**2.1 Which group has the orbits 28 + 35.** Sp6(F2) is transitive on the 63 nonzero vectors (verified). The decomposition 28 + 35 is the orbit decomposition of the subgroup O6+(2) ≅ S8, the stabiliser of the plus-type form Q, into nonsingular and singular points. It is not "the classical orbit decomposition of Sp6(F2) on isotropic subspaces". Consequences that matter for arithmetic: an octad structure on a 6-dimensional kernel K is the same thing as a plus-type quadratic form on K (36 choices for each symplectic structure), and a Frobenius that permutes an octad lies in a conjugate of S8. Only 981820 of the 1451520 elements of Sp6(F2), i.e. 67.6 %, lie in some conjugate of S8; the remaining 32.4 % (all elements of order 9 and some of orders 3, 4, 6, 8, 12) preserve no plus-type form and permute no octad.

**2.2 The identity 28 + 35 = 63 is not a miracle of N = 8.** For every even N the nonzero even subsets of an N-set modulo complement number 2^{N−2} − 1 (N = 6: 15; N = 8: 28 + 35; N = 10: 45 + 210 = 255; N = 12: 66 + 495 + 462 = 1023; verified), because they are the nonzero vectors of the (N−2)-dimensional space 𝟙^⊥/𝟙. What is special at N = 8 is (i) 4 | N, so Q = |x|/2 is well defined and S8 acts orthogonally, and (ii) the exceptional isomorphism S8 ≅ O6+(2) that makes S8 the *whole* orthogonal group; for N = 12, S12 is a small subgroup of O10+(2).

**2.3 The genus-3 family y² + y = x⁷ + λ5x⁵ + λ3x³ + λ1x is the wrong family.** Three independent reasons, all computed:

* It is not supersingular. For all eight coefficient vectors in F2³ the L-polynomial over F2 was computed from N1, N2, N3 (and checked against N4, N5, N6); e.g. y² + y = x⁷ has L(T) = 1 − 2T³ + 8T⁶. The 2-adic Newton polygon has slopes 1/3, 1/3, 1/3, 2/3, 2/3, 2/3 in every case: p-rank 0 (as for every Artin–Schreier curve with one pole), but supersingularity would need all slopes 1/2. The supersingular Artin–Schreier curves of van der Geer–van der Vlugt are y² + y = Σ ai x^{2^i+1}; the exponent 7 = 4 + 2 + 1 has 2-weight 3 and is not of that shape.
* 2Tr(λ7x⁷) is not a Z/4-quadratic form: the polar form Tr(f(x+y) + f(x) + f(y)) is not additive in x (1344 failures out of 4096 triples over F16), whereas for x³, x⁵, x⁹ it is bilinear. So there is no linearised polar operator T', no F2-linear kernel K, and no Gauss sum of a quadratic form to which the Brown-invariant machinery of Parts III–IV applies. The Weil sums of x⁷ are governed by a cubic, not quadratic, form over F2, which is the arithmetic reason behind the bounded variance ratio observed at d = 8 in Part II, Section 9, if the correspondence "Legendre degree d ↔ exponent d − 1" of Parts I–III persists (this correspondence has not been re-derived here).
* Consequently there is no "polarised octic B8" attached to this family. The octad geometry needs a kernel of F2-dimension 6, i.e. a linearised polar operator of 2-degree 6 after clearing roots, which in the Gold family means exponents 3, 5, 9.

## 3. The right home: the genus-4 Gold family

Consider y² + y = λ9x⁹ + λ5x⁵ + λ3x³ + λ1x over Fq, q = 2^k. All exponents are of Gold type 2^i + 1, so the curves are supersingular (van der Geer–van der Vlugt) of genus 4, and by the derivation of Part III the Z/4-form 𝔮(x) = Tw(x) + 2Tr(λ9x⁹ + λ5x⁵ + λ3x³ + λ1x) has polar form 2Tr(y T'(x)) with

T'(x) = λ9x⁸ + λ5x⁴ + λ3x² + x + (λ3x)^{1/2} + (λ5x)^{1/4} + (λ9x)^{1/8}.

(This formula is the exact analogue of the quartic operator of Part III; it has not been re-derived from the Witt-vector trace in this memo and should be, as step 1 of any programme.) Then T'^8 is a 2-linearised polynomial of 2-degree 6 with 2-associate

ℓ(z) = λ9⁸z⁶ + λ5⁸z⁵ + λ3⁸z⁴ + z³ + λ3⁴z² + λ5²z + λ9,

self-reciprocal up to scaling, so for λ9 ≠ 0 the kernel K = ker T' has F2-dimension exactly 6 and the coset T'^{-1}(1) has 64 points. The octad model of Section 1 predicts the shape of the coset: 8 roots of an octic B8(u) with vanishing u⁷-coefficient (Σ ri = 0) and 56 triad totals ri + rj + rk, with K \ 0 = 28 duads ∪ 35 tetrads.

**An explicit octad exists** (verified in `genus_check.py`, Part B): for λ9 = λ5 = λ3 = 1 over F2, T'(x)⁸ = Tr_{128/2}(x), so K is the trace-zero hyperplane of F128 and T'^{-1}(1) the trace-one coset. With α a normal element of F128 of trace 1 (α = x³ + 1 in the basis mod x⁷ + x + 1 works), the octad is {α, α², …, α⁶⁴, 1}: its sum is 0, all eight points lie in the coset, its 28 duads and 35 tetrads are disjoint and exhaust K \ 0, and the coset is exactly the 8 roots plus the 56 triads. The octic is B8(u) = (u + 1)·mα(u) = u⁸ + u⁶ + u + 1 (coefficient of u⁷ is 0), and Frobenius permutes the octad as a 7-cycle fixing 1, class 7 1 of S8.

So the genus-4 analogue of the hexad theorem of Part III is at least *sometimes* true. The next point shows that it is not always true, which is the first genuinely new phenomenon.

## 4. The octad obstruction (new, computed)

Frobenius Fq-Frob = (x ↦ x^q) acts F2-linearly on K. If a Frobenius-stable octad exists, Frobenius permutes its 8 points and therefore acts on K as an element of S8 in its 6-dimensional representation on V. For coefficients in F2 the Frobenius x ↦ x² acts on K as the companion matrix Cℓ of ℓ(z) (K ≅ F2[z]/(ℓ) as F2[z]-module), and the Fq-Frobenius as Cℓ^k. Comparing the F2[z]-module (K, Cℓ^k) with the modules of the 22 classes of S8 on V (complete invariant: ranks of p(g)^j for all irreducible p; the 22 classes give 20 module types, since 4 2 2 and 4 1⁴, and 2⁴ and 2² 1⁴, act on V with the same elementary divisors) gives, for λ9 = 1:

| (λ5, λ3) | ℓ(z) | order of Frob₂ on K | k (mod order) for which an octad is *possible* | k for which **no** Frobenius-stable octad exists |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 0): y² + y = x⁹ + λ1x | z⁶ + z³ + 1 = Φ9 | 9 | 9 only (class 1⁸) | 1, …, 8 (mod 9) |
| (0, 1) | (z² + z + 1)(z⁴ + z³ + z² + z + 1) | 15 | all k (classes 5 3, 5 1³, 3 1⁵, 1⁸) | none |
| (1, 0) | (z² + z + 1)³ | 12 | 3, 9 (class 4 4), 6 (2⁴ or 2² 1⁴), 12 (1⁸) | 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11 (mod 12) |
| (1, 1) | Φ7 = (z³ + z + 1)(z³ + z² + 1) | 7 | all k (class 7 1, explicit octad above) | none |

"Possible" means the necessary condition (Frobenius module realised in S8) holds; it is sufficient in the row (1, 1) where the octad was constructed, and in general still requires a Frobenius-stable choice of the base point r1 in the affine coset. The order-9 row was also checked by brute force: K = {x ∈ F512 : x⁶⁴ + x⁸ + x = 0} has 64 elements and Frobenius has a single orbit of length 63 on K \ 0, order 9, and S8 has no element of order 9.

**Consequence.** In genus 2 every λ carries a hexad (Part III, hexad theorem) because S6 = Sp4(F2): whatever Frobenius does on the four-dimensional kernel, it is a permutation of six things. In genus 4 the Frobenius on K lands in a group of order 1451520 (if, as the self-reciprocal shape of ℓ suggests, it preserves a symplectic form on K; this is the genus-4 version of the "Frobenius-stable symplectic forms" count of Part III and has not been proved here), of which only a third of the elements, by Chebotarev heuristics 67.6 % of the parameters λ if the monodromy is all of Sp6(F2), permute an octad. The genus-4 theory therefore has two regimes, **octad parameters** (Frobenius conjugate into S8: an octic B8 exists, and the hexad formula of Part IV should have an octad analogue with signs ε = +1 at the 8 roots and a sign to be determined at the 56 triads) and **non-octad parameters** (Frobenius of order 9, or of orders 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 outside every conjugate of S8: no B8 at all, and the Gauss sums must be handled directly on the 64-point coset with its symplectic, not permutation, symmetry). The curve y² + y = x⁹ + λ1x over Fq with 3 ∤ k is the simplest non-octad example.

### 4.1 The genus-4 Frobenius table (computed, `ore_algebra_k6.py`, `genus4_class_stats.py`, `sp6_module_types.py`)

**Method.** In the twisted polynomial ring Fq{τ} (τc = c²τ) the kernel polynomial is P = τ⁶ + λ5⁸τ⁵ + λ3⁸τ⁴ + τ³ + λ3⁴τ² + λ5²τ + 1 (λ9 = 1). For g ∈ F2[z], dim ker g(Frob_q)|K = τ-degree of the right gcd of P and g(τ^k), because the right ideal (P, g(τ^k)) is principal with generator whose kernel is the intersection of the kernels. The ranks of p(Frob_q)^j for all irreducible p are therefore computable by right Euclid in Fq{τ}, with no field extension, and they determine the elementary divisors of the F2[z]-module K, i.e. the GL6(F2)-conjugacy class of Frobenius. The implementation was checked against the companion-matrix computation of Section 4 for all λ ∈ F2², q = 2, 4, 8 (identical invariants) and passes ring and division tests. Independently, the GL6(F2)-types of all 1451520 elements of Sp6(F2) were computed with numpy (`sp6_module_types.json`): Sp6(F2) has 24 GL-types, of which 20 occur in S8 (the 22 classes of S8 give 20 module types on V: 4 2 2 ≡ 4 1⁴ and 2⁴ ≡ 2² 1⁴ coincide) and 22 in O6−(2).

**Results for all (λ5, λ3) ∈ Fq², q = 2 … 512** (`genus4_class_stats.txt`, `genus4_counts.json`; q = 512 is 262144 parameters, 4.5 minutes):

| q | octad parameters | proportion |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 / 4 | 0.500 |
| 4 | 12 / 16 | 0.750 |
| 8 | 48 / 64 | 0.750 |
| 16 | 200 / 256 | 0.781 |
| 32 | 792 / 1024 | 0.773 |
| 64 | 3240 / 4096 | 0.791 |
| 128 | 13008 / 16384 | 0.794 |
| 256 | 51544 / 65536 | 0.787 |
| 512 | 207064 / 262144 | 0.790 |

* **Every Frobenius type that occurs is a type of Sp6(F2)**, at every q (0 exceptions among 349 000 parameters), and by q = 512 all 24 types of Sp6(F2) occur. This is strong evidence for Q1 (Frobenius on K is symplectic for an intrinsic form) and Q2 (the monodromy of the family is all of Sp6(F2)).
* **The per-type frequencies at q = 512 match the Chebotarev prediction count(type)/|Sp6(F2)| to three digits** for every type above 0.5 %: e.g. 7 1: 0.1445 vs 1/7 = 0.1429; 8: 0.1230 vs 1/8; order 9 (non-octad): 0.1109 vs 1/9; 5 2 1: 0.1004 vs 1/10; 4 3 1, 6 1 1 and the non-octad type (z²+z+1)³: 0.084 vs 1/12; 5 3: 0.0660 vs 1/15.
* **The octad proportion converges to 0.790, not to 0.676.** The relevant prediction is the fraction of Sp6(F2) whose *GL6-type* occurs in S8, which is 1146880/1451520 = 0.7901, not the fraction 981820/1451520 = 0.6764 of elements lying in a conjugate of S8 *inside* Sp6(F2). The reason is that an octad brings its own symplectic form (the polar form of Q = |x|/2) which need not coincide with the intrinsic form of K; a Frobenius-stable octad exists as soon as Frobenius is GL-conjugate into S8. The 165060 elements of Sp6(F2) in the difference are those whose GL-type is an S8 type but which preserve no plus-type form polarising to the intrinsic ω. (If one insists on octads whose form is ω, 0.676 is the right number; the data decide for 0.790, which is one more sign that the octad is not intrinsic to ω.)
* **Non-octad parameters** are exactly the four GL-types (z⁶+z³+1) [order 9, 1/9 of all], (z²+z+1)³ [order 12 or 3, 1/12], (z²+z+1)(z²+z+1)² [1/72] and (z²+z+1)³ semisimple [order 3, 1/648]: all have characteristic polynomial a power of Φ3 or Φ9, i.e. Frobenius acts through the field F4 or F64 structure of K. Total predicted 0.2099, observed 0.210 at q = 512.
* **Unlike Table 2 of Part III, the counts are not quadratic polynomials in q on a fixed parity of k.** Interpolation through the three largest q of either parity fails on the remaining points for every type; the deviations from the main term |C| q²/|G| are of size O(q) (q = 512: 7 1 gives 37885 against 37449.1; 8 gives 32244 against 32768; order 9 gives 29061 against 29127.1). The lower-order terms presumably depend on k modulo the orders 3, 7, 9, 15 that occur, or are genuine Frobenius traces of a non-constant local system; nine values of q cannot decide this. This is a second genuine difference from genus 2, where the eleven counts are exact polynomials.

## 5. Programme

1. **Derive T' for genus 4** from the Witt-vector trace, as in Part III, and confirm numerically that ker T' has dimension 6 and that T'^{-1}(1) has 64 points for every λ with λ9 ≠ 0 (small q).
2. **Frobenius class on K for all λ ∈ Fq³, q = 2, 4, 8, 16, 32.** No field extensions are needed: for 2-linearised polynomials over Fq, ker L ⊆ ker M iff L right-divides M in the twisted polynomial ring Fq{τ}, so the dimension of ker g(Frob_q) ∩ K equals the τ-degree of the right gcd of T'^8 and g(τ^k) for g ∈ F2[z]. This gives the module invariant of Frobenius on K, hence (i) whether λ is an octad parameter, (ii) the S8 class when it is, (iii) the conjugacy class in GL6(F2) (or Sp6(F2)) when it is not, and the counts of each class as functions of q, which is the genus-4 analogue of Table 2 of Part III.
3. **Intrinsic symplectic form.** Decide whether K carries a canonical nondegenerate alternating form preserved by Frobenius (candidates: the polar form of the Witt form restricted suitably, or the Weil-type pairing on the Dieudonné module of the supersingular Jacobian), and whether the octad quadratic form Q is intrinsic (Q(duad) = 1, Q(tetrad) = 0 looks like a half-Witt value, compare the sixteen-point lemma of Part IV).
4. **Octad formula.** For octad parameters, test numerically s_{2m}(λ) = q^m Σ_{w ∈ T'^{-1}(1) ∩ F_{q^m}} ε(w) i^{𝔮^{(m)}(w)} with ε(root) = +1 and ε(triad) ∈ {±1} to be determined; the first feasible case is q = 2, m = 7 for λ = (1, 1, 1) (sum over F_{2^{14}}, all 64 coset points rational). For non-octad parameters test the coset form of the hexad formula directly (Part IV, `part4_relative_reduction.py`), which never used the roots.
5. **Trace parity and sign rules.** The trace-parity theorem of Part IV (Tr(w) ≡ mk + dim(K ∩ F_{q^m}) mod 2) was proved from the shift identity and complex conjugation alone and should hold verbatim for genus 4; the sixteen-point lemma becomes a sixty-four-point lemma on 8 + 56 points with S8 (octad case) or Sp6(F2) (general case) symmetry.
6. Only after 1–5: which Legendre-interval degree the family x⁹ governs (if the pattern of Parts I–III persists it is d = 10, and d = 8 is governed by the non-quadratic x⁷ sums of Section 2.3), and whether a closed form of the type X = q⁶ + q⁵ exists there.

## 6. Open questions recorded

* Q1. Is the Frobenius on K = ker T' (genus 4) always symplectic for an intrinsic form, so that its class lives in Sp6(F2) (30 classes) rather than GL6(F2)?
* Q2. Are all 30 classes of Sp6(F2) realised as λ varies over F̄q, with counts polynomial in q (Chebotarev for the monodromy of the family)?
* Q3. For octad parameters, what is the sign ε at the 56 triads in the octad formula, and does B8 have a closed form in λ analogous to B = λ5²u⁶ + λ3²u² + λ5u + 1?
* Q4. For non-octad parameters, what replaces the sixteen-point lemma? Verified: every element of Sp6(F2) preserves at least one of the 64 quadratic forms with polar form ⟨,⟩, and the 469700 elements that preserve no plus-type form all preserve a minus-type form, i.e. lie in a conjugate of O6−(2) (order 51840, the Weyl group of E6). So a non-octad Frobenius still has an orthogonal structure, that of a minus-type form with 27 singular points (the 27 lines on a cubic surface), and the natural guess is that the sixteen-point lemma is replaced by a lemma on the ⟨Frobenius⟩-orbits of the 64-point coset organised by that form.
